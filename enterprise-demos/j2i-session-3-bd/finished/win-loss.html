What we see

Small sample: one call, eleven inbound leads

1. Half the dropped leads were never buyers

Four of the eight (a vendor, a graduate, a recruiter and a student) fall outside the ideal client, and all four came through open channels (LinkedIn DMs and the conference scan). Two of the seven conference rows were non-buyers.

2. The rest are small, early or not yet

Kessler (25 staff, unsure about AI), Ridgeback (no budget), Meridian (asked for November) and the marketplace post (a few thousand dollars, two weeks) each lack size, budget or timing. This matches the CLAUDE.md note that small firms "kick the tires".

3. The strong leads share one trigger: a dated recompete

Harborline (March recompete, December solicitation) and Bluefield (recompete in Q1) are federal contractors with a deadline. Both came with proposal pain. The three leads we would pursue are all in the federal market.

4. Every objection is about trust and control, none is about price

All six objections concern invented facts, controlled data, approval gates, an unused tool or timing. Price never came up, but it was also never discussed. The earlier tool's fabricated contract number is the single most important fact on the call.

5. No competitor was named

The buyer named none. The only alternatives were an unnamed AI proposal tool they had dropped and personal ChatGPT use. GovDash, Sweetspot and APEX Accelerators, from CLAUDE.md, were not mentioned by anyone. Nothing here supports a claim about any of them.

6. The first read of a lead was off

The LinkedIn DM said "about 140 people" and a single task order. On the call the COO corrected this to 138 people and two task orders. The playback step caught it.

What we should change

Suggestions for J2i to confirm

Targeting

  • Aim at one trigger. Put veteran-owned federal contractors with a recompete or solicitation date in the next six months at the front. Where those dates come from: [TO CONFIRM source].
  • Ask for company type before booking. A single check on the conference form, and a quick look at each DM, would remove the four non-buyers before anyone spends time on them.
  • Nurture the small and early ones, don't run discovery. Kessler, Ridgeback and Meridian each get a helpful email drafted for a person to send, with a calendar reminder: November for Meridian, 90 days for Ridgeback.
  • Decide the marketplace post deliberately. Reply only if J2i has a small fixed-scope offer. Whether it does: [TO CONFIRM].

Messages

  • Lead with "nothing invented". Say that every line points back to the source page or document, and that a person checks it. This answers objections 2 and 3 directly. Don't claim it until the build partner confirms it can do this: [TO CONFIRM].
  • Raise data handling in the first conversation. Say plainly that we work only in tools the client's IT security approves, and invite the IT security lead to the scoping call (objections 4 and 5).
  • Describe the work as finding and checking, not writing. "AI writes your proposal" is the claim the earlier tool made and broke.
  • Tie the offer to their calendar. Quote the solicitation date back to them (objection 6) and show what is possible before it lands.
  • Use shadow AI as an opening. Personal ChatGPT use is a risk the client already knows about. A short training-and-policy offer would answer it. Whether J2i wants to offer that: [TO CONFIRM].

The log itself

  • Ask "what else have you looked at?" on every call, and get the name of the earlier tool from Harborline. Add a competitor only once a buyer names it.
  • Record real won and lost outcomes as deals close. Until then, treat these patterns as hypotheses.
