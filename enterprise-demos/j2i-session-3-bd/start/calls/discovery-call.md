# Discovery call · Harborline Systems Group

FICTIONAL. Written for the Session 3 demo in J2i's discovery method (Session 1: playback, invite correction,
SPIN, never solution, one commitment). Harborline and everyone on the call are invented.

- Date: last week, 45 minutes, video
- Harborline: Dana Whitfield (COO), Ray Alvarez (Capture and Proposal Lead)
- J2i: call lead, second chair
- Recording consent given at the start

---

**[00:00] J2i:** Before we start, are you both fine with us recording so we can send an accurate summary?
**Dana:** Yes, fine.

**[00:30] J2i:** Let me play back what we understand, and correct me where I'm wrong. Harborline is a
service-disabled veteran-owned business, about 140 people, mostly IT service desk and field support for the
VA. Your main task order ends in the spring and will be competed again, and the proposal team is small.
**Dana:** Close. It's 138 people, and it's two task orders, not one. The big one ends in March. The smaller one
has an option year we expect them to exercise, but nobody has told us in writing.
**J2i:** What do you want to make sure we get to today?
**Dana:** Whether any of this AI stuff actually helps a team like Ray's, or if we're buying another tool
nobody opens.

**[03:10] Situation**
**J2i:** Ray, walk us through how a proposal gets built today.
**Ray:** The solicitation drops. I read the whole thing, usually 80 to 120 pages, and build a compliance
matrix in Excel by hand. That alone is two to three days. Then I chase subject matter experts for write-ups,
pull past performance from old proposals on the shared drive, and write the technical volume myself mostly.
**J2i:** How many proposals a year?
**Ray:** Twelve to fifteen we actually submit. We pass on more than that because we don't have the hours.
**J2i:** Roughly how many do you pass on?
**Ray:** Maybe another ten. Some of those we'd have had a real shot at.

**[09:40] Problem**
**J2i:** Where does it hurt most?
**Ray:** Finding things. Our best past performance write-ups are scattered across three drives and people's
laptops. I rewrite the same company overview every time because I can't find the good version.
**Dana:** And the experts. Our engineers are billable. Every hour they spend on a proposal is an hour off a
contract, and they hate it.
**J2i:** You said "the good version". How often does that happen?
**Ray:** Every proposal. Easily a day each time, just hunting.

**[16:20] Implication**
**J2i:** What happens if the March recompete goes badly?
**Dana:** It's about 40 percent of our revenue. We'd be laying people off.
**J2i:** And the proposals you pass on?
**Dana:** That's growth we never see. We've been flat for two years.
**J2i:** Has anything been tried?
**Ray:** We trialled one of the AI proposal tools last year. It wrote confident nonsense about our past
performance, made up a contract number. After that nobody trusted it.
**Dana:** That's my worry. I'm not putting something in front of a contracting officer that invents facts.

**[24:50] Need-payoff**
**J2i:** If the compliance matrix and the past performance search took hours instead of days, what changes?
**Ray:** I'd bid on more. Honestly the ones we pass on.
**Dana:** And the engineers would give us one review hour instead of a day of writing.
**J2i:** If you could fix one thing in the next 90 days, which?
**Ray:** Getting the March recompete right. Everything else is second.
**Dana:** Agreed. And whatever we do has to work on our own data. Some of it is controlled, so it can't go
into just any tool.

**[31:15] Constraints**
**J2i:** Who else would need to say yes to work like this?
**Dana:** Me for anything under fifty thousand. Our CEO signs above that. Our IT security lead has to approve
any tool touching contract data.
**J2i:** Timing?
**Ray:** The draft solicitation is expected in December. Final in January. Proposals usually due 30 days later.
**J2i:** Tools today?
**Ray:** Microsoft 365, SharePoint, Excel, a bit of ChatGPT on personal accounts, which I know isn't allowed.

**[38:00] Close**
**J2i:** We won't solution today. Within 24 hours you'll have a one-page AI Opportunity Map from us, in your
words, with what we heard and where we think the leverage is. It's yours to keep either way.
**Dana:** That works. If it's useful, we'd want to move fast. December is close.

**[44:30] End**
