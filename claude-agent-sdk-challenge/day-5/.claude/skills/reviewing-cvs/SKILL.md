# Reviewing CVs (UK)

The full rule list, with sources, is in [reference/uk-cv-rules.md](reference/uk-cv-rules.md).

## Workflow

Copy this checklist into your thinking and tick it off:

```
- [ ] 1. List the ad's essential requirements, in the ad's own words
- [ ] 2. Match each one to a CV line, quoted exactly, or mark it a gap
- [ ] 3. Pick the 3 CV lines that matter most for THIS job
- [ ] 4. Rewrite those 3 lines (rules below; examples in reference/examples.md)
- [ ] 5. Run check_cv_bullets on the rewrites
- [ ] 6. Fix every issue it reports, then run it again until it says PASS
- [ ] 7. Run check_evidence on every quote you'll use (strengths and "from_cv"), and fix
         or drop any it reports. A rewrite may only say what its quote says.
```

## The rules that matter most

1. **Lead with the result, not the duty.** "Cut reporting from 4 hours to 30 minutes", not
   "Responsible for weekly reporting".
2. **Start with a strong past-tense verb**: Built, Cut, Led, Launched, Rebuilt, Automated.
   Never "Responsible for", "Helped", "Worked on", "Assisted with".
3. **Never add a number the CV doesn't have.** If the CV line has no figure, show the result
   in words. Inventing a figure is the worst thing a CV review can do.
4. **Use the ad's words** where the CV genuinely matches, so a recruiter or an applicant
   tracking system sees the match.
5. **One CV line in, one bullet out.** Don't merge two roles or two achievements.
6. **UK spelling** (organise, analyse, optimise, behaviour) and under 30 words per bullet.

## Career changers

If the CV is from a different field, lead with transferable results that the ad asks for,
and say plainly in the gaps what they still need. Don't dress old job titles up as new ones.

## Cover note opener

One sentence: who they are in the ad's terms, plus their single strongest result from the
CV. No "I am writing to apply", no invented motives.
