# LinkedIn post

> One post, shipped at the end of Week 3. The goal is a hiring manager clicking
> through to the repo, so the link and the number matter more than the prose.

---

## Structure

**Hook (1 line).** A number or a specific failure. Not "I'm excited to share".

**What you built (2-3 lines).** The problem first, then the system. A reader
who does not know your domain should still follow it.

**The number (1-2 lines).** One result, with the method in the same breath.
"93% category accuracy on 40 cases I labelled by hand" beats "great results".

**The honest bit (1-2 lines).** What it does not do, or what you got wrong
first. This is the line that makes the rest believable.

**Link (1 line).** Repo, and the demo video if it is public.

---

## Draft

```
I spent three weeks building <thing>, and the hardest part was not the model.

<Problem, 2 lines.>

<What it does, 2 lines.>

<The number, with method. 1-2 lines.>

<What surprised me / what it still cannot do. 1-2 lines.>

Code and a 3-minute demo: <link>
```

---

**Before posting**

- [ ] The link works from a logged-out browser.
- [ ] The README's first screen makes sense to someone who arrived cold.
- [ ] No employer-confidential data in the repo, the video, or the post.
- [ ] The number in the post matches the number in the eval report.
